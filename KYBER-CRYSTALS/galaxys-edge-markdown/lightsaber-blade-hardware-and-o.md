- U2 TD6817 Techcode Step-Down Regulator Converts the power from the batteries to 3.3V used to drive the LEDs in the blade; 300uA quiescent current, 100nA in shutdown mode
- U3 U1RJ MicroOne 3.3V LDO Voltage Regulator Converts the power from the batteries to 3.3V used to power the microcontroller; 100mA (max) output, 3uA quiescent current
- Q3 Y2 PNP Transistor (SS8550) Provides power to bank 1 of LEDs in blade; GP1
- Q4 Y2 PNP Transistor (SS8550) Provides power to bank 2 of LEDs in blade; GP2
- Q12 Y2 PNP Transistor (SS8550) Provides power to bank 3 of LEDs in blade; GP3
- Q13 Y2 PNP Transistor (SS8550) Provides power to bank 4 of LEDs in blade; GP4
- Q5 A2H N-Channel MOSFET (SI2302) Allows power to flow through all GREEN LEDs in the blade
- Q6 A2H N-Channel MOSFET (SI2302) Allows power to flow through all RED LEDs in the blade
- Q7 A2H N-Channel MOSFET (SI2302) Allows power to flow through all BLUE LEDs in the blade
- Q8 (*) Y2 PNP Transistor (SS8550) A buffer between the blade and hilt on the data line; (*) not present in the first version of the PCB
- D10 SS34 Schottky Diode Reduces the voltage through RED LEDs making them slightly dimmer than if it were not present.
- L1 2R2 2.2uH Inductor Used with U2 to provide power to LEDs
- R52 150K Resistor Used with R53 to program the output voltage of U2
- R53 680K Resistor Used with R52 to program the output voltage of U2
- R86, R88, R89, R103 1K Resistor Controls current through the base of PNP transistors Q3, Q4, Q12, Q13
- FB1 Likely a Ferrite Bead (FB) Filters high frequency noise on the battery power rail
- R1, R2, R3, R4 0 ohm resistors (link) nothing; acts like a wire (**)
- ** so why include them? not sure. best guess is they were designed into the PCB so they could add resistors (same value for all 4) to dim the blade, which would extend battery life, but ultimately decided against that and stuck 0 ohm resistors in there.
- NOTE: newer blades have different MOSFETs for Q5, Q6, Q7

- Blade Operation Notes
- The lightsaber blade contains its own microcontroller which controls the operation of the blade. It receives commands from the hilt via a single data line labeled "I/P" (white wire) on the blade's logic PCB.
- Power to the blade comes directly from the batteries in the hilt. All voltage regulation happens on the blade's logic PCB.
- There are two voltage regulators, both output 3.3 volts. The smaller regulator (U3) powers the microcontroller. The larger regulator (U2) powers the LEDs of the blade and can be enabled/disabled by the microcontroller.
- Why two voltage regulators? Not certain. Perhaps the larger one (U2) has a high quiescent current which would drain batteries more quickly if left on all the time, but is needed for its high current rating versus the smaller voltage regulator.

- Blade Command & Kyber Crystal IDs, Timings, And Stuff
- There are two commands used to tell the blade to ignite. One command is used by Savi's Workshop hilts, the other command is used by legacy hilts.
- Each command carries with it a 4-bit value (0-15) that identifies the hilt the blade is connected to.
- Kyber crystals also contain an ID that is used by holcorons and Savi's Workshop hilts to identify the crystal. These values can also be mapped to a value between 0 and 15[*].
- * There are 17 known kyber crystal RFIDs, 3 are 'special' (snoke, 8-ball vader, 8-ball yoda) and have numbers outside the base 16. cyan and and orange are not official kyber crystal colors, but there is room for them in the base 16 mapping.

- STANDARD Savi's Workshop Lightsaber Power Off Sequence
- At the start of the power off sequence, segment 4 is PWM'd at 66% for 75ms, then drops to 33% for 85ms, then turns off completely.
- When segment 4 drops to 33% PWM, segment 3 drops to 66% PWM. This sequence continues through all four segments until all four segments are off.
- The fade-out sequence of each segment overlaps its neighbor to produce a smooth blade contraction effect.
-

- The Diode in Red
- The red channel in a blade has a Schottky diode in series with it with the reference designator D10.
- This drops an extra 0.2 to 0.3 volts of electricity, which lowers the amount of current flowing through the red LEDs in the blade.
- If you want to modify the amount of red color in your blade, replacing D10 is an easy way to do it.
- To increase the red, remove the diode and replace it with a small piece of wire.
- To decrease the red, you could replace it with a diode that has a higher voltage drop or you could replace it with a resistor.
